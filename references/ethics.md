# Ethics and the disclosure test

## The spectrum

```
ethical persuasion  →  manipulation  →  coercion / dark patterns
```

- **Ethical persuasion** — transparent, mutual value, autonomy intact, claims true.
- **Manipulation** — covert intent, selective framing that hides material drawbacks,
  exploiting a bias for asymmetric gain.
- **Coercion / dark patterns** — the choice is restricted, deceptive, or taken against the
  person's own interest.

The line is not about which technique you use. Anchoring, scarcity, and social proof appear
on both sides of it. The line is about whether the person could have said no with full
knowledge.

## The disclosure test

> If the audience read an accurate, plain-language explanation of the mechanism being used,
> would they still willingly proceed?

- **Green** — yes. Ethical. Proceed.
- **Red** — no. Manipulative. Replace the tactic.

Run it on the mechanism, not the wording. A clearer sentence over an artificial deadline is
still an artificial deadline.

## Automatic reds

| Pattern | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Fabricated reviews, testimonials, or user counts | The proof is a lie; it borrows trust it did not earn |
| Invented deadlines or "3 left in stock" | Manufactures loss aversion that does not exist |
| Fees or conditions revealed after the commitment | The decision was made on false information |
| Cancellation or opt-out made harder than signup | Autonomy is obstructed at the moment it matters most |
| Confirmshaming ("No thanks, I prefer losing money") | Punishes the decline instead of respecting it |
| A hidden default that selects the paid option | Consent is assumed, not given |
| A recommendation that serves the seller and harms the buyer | Interests are misaligned by design |

## Keeping it green

1. **Keep the exit as easy as the entrance.** Cancel, downgrade, and decline stay one obvious
   step away.
2. **Show the real trade-off, including the weakness.** The objection the audience will find
   anyway is cheaper to raise yourself.
3. **Use only numbers you can defend.** If the stat would embarrass you in a room with the
   customer, cut it.
4. **Align the action with the person's own values**, not with yours disguised as theirs.

## When the user asks for a red

Say plainly that the tactic fails the test, name the mechanism it exploits, and offer the
green variant that reaches the same business goal. A real deadline instead of an invented
one. A named customer instead of an anonymous "thousands." A clearer price instead of a
hidden fee.
